## Cases

**1. Hans Jørgen Wiberg — Be My Eyes (Denmark; has RP)** [verified-fetch]
First artefact: a pitch, not code — idea from FaceTime, pitched at a Danish Startup Weekend 2012. First collaborator: "I was lucky enough to be able to form a team of interested programmers" at that weekend; studio Robocat built it as volunteers. First money: Danish Blind Society backing + Velux Foundations grant (~$300k). Later angels, $2.8M A 2020, $6.1M Jan 2025. First distribution: Danish Blind Society network + press; 13,000 volunteers in days; the hard side was reaching blind users: "we want to reach out to the blind people but it is not that easy." Status 2026: 1M+ BLV users, Be My AI, Ray-Ban Meta integration, Apple Cultural Impact award Dec 2025. Quote: "The blind are unable to read publications such as tech crunch." https://impakter.com/bemyeyes-the-founder-interview/ · https://techcrunch.com/2015/01/16/be-my-eyes/ · https://veluxfonden.dk/en/content/app-lets-people-lend-their-eyes-blind

**2. Stephen Hicks — Oxford smart glasses → OXSIGHT (UK; Australian-born, sighted)** [verified-fetch status; origin partial]

First money: Royal Society Brian Mercer award £50k (2013), Google Impact Challenge UK 2014 People's Choice £500k with RNIB. Status: OXSIGHT LTD entered **creditors' voluntary liquidation 26 Sep 2023**. https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/10084672/insolvency · https://www.australiantimes.co.uk/news/aussie-wins-uk-google-award-for-blind-smart-glasses/

## Cross-case patterns

1. **The first artefact is a demo that provokes a reaction, not a product** (1, 3, 5, 6, 9, 2, 11). *The rp-simulator is already this.*
2. **First collaborators come from a convening, never cold outreach** — Startup Weekend (1), hackathon (3), NGO academy (7), MBA course (6), thesis + old friend (5), institute that lent a room (9), visiting professorship (8), accelerator cohort (10 Velasquez). Find the room, then the person.
3. **First money is non-dilutive and mission-aligned; VC is round two or three** (1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 10). Only Glidance (4) opened with equity — with a 20-year Microsoft/Guide Dogs record behind it.
4. **Blindness organisations are the first distribution channel, community media the second** (1, 2, 6, 7, 9; 4, 5, 11).
5. **Lived-experience founders pair with a builder/operator; sighted founders pair with a blind lead user** — the pair exists before the money (all cases).
6. **Software shipped in months; hardware took 5–10 years and is either dead or still pre-revenue** (Be My Eyes / Seeing AI / Envision app: 1–3 yrs; OxSight, AI Suitcase, Hope Tech, Glide: 5–10 yrs).
7. **Pre-orders / deposits work as community-underwritten validation** (4, 5, 9).
8. **The employer as incubator is a legitimate path** (3, 8, and Soundscape before Glide).
Confidence high on 1–5; medium on 6–8 (small n).

## Anti-patterns

- **Award-led hardware with no commercial engine — OxSight.** £550k+ in prizes, spinout, RNIB endorsement; liquidated 2023. Prizes buy prototypes, not a sales channel.
- **Grant-cycle death.** GDI Hub founders: "if you keep being dependent on grants, that's not sustainable"; "The moment the big funder pulls out, everything falls flat." https://www.disabilityinnovation.com/blog/profit-for-impact
- **Cash-flow fragility even with VC + government money — BindiMaps.**
- **Pricing for the wrong market — Hope Tech.**
- **Research model gives impact without a product — AI Suitcase.**

## What the patient-innovation literature adds

- Of 500 rare-disease patients/carers, 53% made a solution, 8% expert-validated as novel, only 32% shared it with anyone and 6% with a clinician; higher education doubled odds of innovating. `[peer-reviewed]` Oliveira, Zejnilovic, Canhão, von Hippel 2015 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4404234/
- Commercialisation rate ~15%; 82% built because market products "did not fully meet a need". `[peer-reviewed]` Jacinto, Oliveira, Canhão 2021 https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmed.2021.647388/full
- Named barriers: "lack of resources, skills or access to the process of approval and commercialisation"; recommended role for researchers/clinicians is to support, not take over. `[peer-reviewed]` Reinius et al. 2022 https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8788234/
- Lead-user theory: lead users face needs the market will feel "months or years in the future" and "often attempt to fill the need they experience". `[peer-reviewed]` von Hippel 1986. Conradie et al. 2014 (18 cases of disabled persons as lead users): lead users are "easier to find after innovation has already occurred" — having built something is what makes you visible. `[peer-reviewed]` https://www.designsociety.org/download-publication/36273/
- **Read-across `[my-synthesis]`:** the scarce things are a partner with commercialisation skills and a diffusion channel — which the founders above got from a convening and a blindness organisation, not from more building.
