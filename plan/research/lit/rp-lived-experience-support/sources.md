## W2968766491 — Garip & Kamal 2019, BMC Ophthalmology
Systematic review + meta-synthesis of coping with RP. Verified (PMC6693213). `[peer-reviewed/systematic-review/full-text]`
Appraisal: 12 qualitative studies, 7 countries, n=126 (18–85). Downgrade flags: participants predominantly late-stage RP; recruitment via charities (selection toward adaptive/engaged individuals — the review itself notes non-engagers are underrepresented, which is exactly our protagonist).
Findings:
- Diagnosis provokes "shock, fear, panic, disbelief, devastation"; denial and concealment (workplace) are common maladaptive responses. → RQ1, RQ3
- Deferral is documented behaviourally: people delayed cane use despite benefits; overwhelm/fatigue limited engagement with rehabilitation. → RQ1, RQ2
- Early social support eased adaptation: those with "social support from early on in their RP diagnosis" adapted more easily; participants "would have liked the opportunity to talk about their feelings at the time of diagnosis". → RQ3, RQ5
- Support groups helped but were timing/fit-sensitive (age-mismatched group failed one participant). → RQ5
- Clinician communication style (blunt/vague/mild) materially shaped emotional response at diagnosis. → RQ3
- Adaptive coping repertoire: planning ahead, allowing extra time, AT use, humour, downward comparison, acceptance. → RQ1
PARKED: quality scoring method (CASP 8–10/10) — method detail only.

## W3109961591 — Demmin & Silverstein 2020, Clinical Ophthalmology
"Visual Impairment and Mental Health: Unmet Needs and Treatment Options". Verified (PMC7721280). `[peer-reviewed/narrative-review/full-text]` [T1: broad VI, AMD-heavy]
Appraisal: narrative (not systematic); broad-VI population — transferability flag to RP per T1.
Findings:
- Clinically significant depressive symptoms in 10.7–45.2% of VI samples; VI confers 1.6–2.8× depression risk. → RQ6, RQ1
- Up to 91% of those with mental-health problems receive no treatment; in one sample 12% on antidepressants, none receiving counselling. → RQ4
- Barriers: no integrated care, few trained providers, patients prioritising independence over help-seeking. → RQ4, RQ1
- Timing signal: later-life loss may be worse for depression than early-life loss ("lesser need to relearn life skills" when early) — indirect support for early-skill-acquisition value. → RQ2, RQ3
- Screening should capture the patient's "stage of emotional adjustment" — services should be stage-aware. → RQ4
PARKED: intervention options review (CBT, problem-solving) — solution territory.

## W4283156377 — Cross et al 2022, Clinical Ophthalmology
"Retinitis Pigmentosa: Burden of Disease and Current Unmet Needs". Verified (PMC9232096). `[peer-reviewed/systematic-review+expert-interviews/full-text]`
Appraisal: three-stage (lit search, PICOS review, physician interviews EU4+UK). Downgrade flag: physician—not patient—interviews for the qualitative arm; industry-adjacent authorship worth checking at map stage.
Findings:
- 92% of RP patients considered the disease affected their mental health; distress exceeds diabetic retinopathy and AMD comparators. → RQ6, RQ1
- Employment 61.5% vs 83.9% controls; falls further with progression (79.5% low vision → 67.6% blind). → RQ6
- Progression anatomy for RQ6 framing: central vision "relatively preserved in early stages", declining acuity as cone degeneration proceeds; most legally blind by ~40, central vision lost by ~60. → RQ6
- Unmet needs: psychosocial support referrals inconsistent; multidisciplinary care required but patchy; limited clinical guidance published; <6% eligible for gene therapy — "most patients live with this chronic, progressive condition without interventions to change the disease course". → RQ4
- Charles Bonnet hallucinations under-reported due to stigma. → RQ6, RQ1
PARKED: epidemiology/cost detail (economic burden excluded by spot-check ruling).

## W2578863234 — Prem Senthil et al 2017, Eye
"Seeing through their eyes: lived experiences of people with RP". Verified (PMC5437327). `[peer-reviewed/qual-interview/full-text]`
Appraisal: n=23 telephone interviews, Australia (RSB Adelaide + Retina Australia), mean age 55.6, mean disease duration 38.9y. Flags: late-stage skew, charity recruitment.
Findings:
- Five QoL themes: activity limitation (reading, shopping, driving, unfamiliar navigation), health concerns (fear of blindness, falls, progression uncertainty), emotional impact (frustration, anxiety, depression, embarrassment), visual symptoms, coping. → RQ1, RQ6
- Reading loss felt as identity-level loss: "My biggest loss for my own self is reading a novel." → RQ6
- Avoidance-coping documented: "I avoid supermarkets or big shops… I find them very overwhelming." → RQ1
- Dissatisfaction with medical providers' information provision; peer interaction used as coping; little formal-service engagement reported. → RQ3, RQ4, RQ5
- Progression uncertainty is itself a named stressor. → RQ1
PARKED: instrument-development motivation (paper feeds later IRD PRO work).

## W4315489475 — Trott et al 2023, Eye
"Mental health support across the sight loss pathway" (patients, optometrists, ECLOs). Verified (PMC10397192). `[peer-reviewed/qual-interview/full-text]` [T1: mixed eye conditions incl. Stargardt]
Appraisal: n=28 (17 patients, 5 optometrists, 5 ECLOs), UK.
Findings:
- Proactive emotional support absent along the pathway: "Never. I have never been offered any [mental health] support at all"; services that exist go un-signposted — "[The eye hospital] also has a counselling service, but nobody tells you about it" (Stargardt patient). → RQ4, RQ3
- Professional's own verdict: support landscape "dire, utterly dire". → RQ4
- ECLOs deliver real emotional support but coverage is patchy and facility-dependent. → RQ4
- Recommendations: early signposting, standardisation across specialties, coordinated care. → RQ4
PARKED: none.

## W4317605791 — Stolwijk et al 2023, BMC Health Services Research
Referral pathways to low vision services, patients + professionals. Verified (PMC9860223). `[peer-reviewed/qual-interview/full-text]` [T1: AMD/glaucoma/DR, older; not RP]
Appraisal: n=14 patients (mean 74) + 16 professionals, Netherlands, framework analysis.
Findings:
- Referral gated on patient readiness: ophthalmologists "only refer patients if they want to be referred"; newly diagnosed patients defer while hoping for treatment. → RQ4, RQ2 (deferral is co-produced by services)
- Identity/stigma barrier at the service door: "I'm welcomed by a blind woman, so it could get even worse" — services coded as *blindness* institutions repel partial-vision patients. → RQ4, RQ1 (binary-service concept)
- Professionals systematically mis-assume eligibility (age, acuity), overlooking near-vision impairment. → RQ4
- Information asymmetry: professionals believe they inform; patients report late/absent information. → RQ4, RQ3
- Facilitators: felt functional impact, self-advocacy, family support, embedded LVS in hospitals. → RQ4, RQ5
PARKED: clinical decision-support tooling recommendation.

## W4409953404 — Ng et al 2025, Journal of Community Genetics
"'I miss stars, too'": reflexive thematic analysis of r/retinitispigmentosa. Verified (PMC12401853). `[peer-reviewed/qual-online-thematic/full-text]`
Appraisal: 69 posts/comments, 36 users, two threads — small, self-selected, demographics unknown.
Findings:
- Grief for progressive loss extends beyond function to activities, independence, identity: "I miss being able to see stars. Often I weep when thinking about it." → RQ1, RQ6

- Hope↔frustration oscillation around gene therapy news. → RQ1

## W3120423789 — Lange et al 2021, J Patient-Reported Outcomes
VRQoL in severe peripheral field loss (RP + glaucoma), qualitative. Verified (PMC7806695). `[peer-reviewed/qual-interview/full-text]`
Appraisal: n=37 (19 RP), median 61, statutory blindness with PFL; saturation reached. Glaucoma arm = [T1].
Findings:
- Six challenge domains: mobility (dark/crowded/unfamiliar), reading (tracking lines, magnifier trade-offs), driving loss, emotional (frustration, embarrassment, anxiety, depression), social function (face recognition failures, relationship strain), activity avoidance (crowds, sports, hobbies). → RQ6, RQ1
- Activity avoidance appears as a coping mode — matches the dossier's "adaptations that shrink life". → RQ1
- "I'm kind of like a horse with blinders on… I'm easily startled" — the startle/blinders experience of PFL. → RQ6
- Notably absent: preparation strategies for anticipated progression were NOT discussed — the challenge inventory is present-tense. Recorded as an in-source gap. → RQ2 (gap)
PARKED: PRO-instrument implications.

## W2799757981 — Sainohira et al 2018, PLoS ONE
Anxiety/depression factors in RP, Japan. Verified (PMC5912752). `[peer-reviewed/cross-sectional-survey/full-text]`
Appraisal: n=112, five hospitals, HADS + NEI-VFQ25; cross-sectional (no causality).
Findings:
- Anxiety 37%, depression 26% of RP patients. → RQ6
- Depression tracks current visual function (all acuity/field measures) and unemployment; employment protective. → RQ6
- Anxiety does NOT track acuity/function — it associates with general-health and role-limitation perceptions (and is higher among RP-society members). Reading: anxiety is anticipatory/uncertainty-driven rather than impairment-driven. → RQ6, RQ1 (uncertainty concept)
PARKED: instrument subscale detail.

## W4385418592 — Murro et al 2023, Orphanet J Rare Diseases
Multidisciplinary approach to IRDs, diagnosis → initial care. Verified (EPMC PMC10388566). `[peer-reviewed/narrative-review/full-text]`
Appraisal: narrative review with clinical-practice input; prescriptive (what care *should* be) more than descriptive.
Findings:
- Best-practice claim: "Psychological support for patients and caregivers is important at all stages"; genetic counselling before AND after testing; rehabilitation "often begins from the time of diagnosis" and is lifelong, evolving with progression. → RQ3, RQ2
- Named practice gap: the interdisciplinary ideal "remains underimplemented in routine clinical settings" — the recommended pathway largely does not exist in practice. → RQ4
- Coordination role (genetic counselors + low-vision specialists guiding families through stages) is recommended, i.e. a *guided path* is the stated ideal absent in routine care. → RQ4, RQ2
PARKED: pediatric neuropsychiatry specifics.

## W2034934184 — Combs et al 2013, European J Human Genetics
Impact of genetic testing for IRD. Verified (Nature HTML). `[peer-reviewed/qual-interview/full-text]`
Appraisal: n=20 UK family members (14 tested, 5 declined), telephone interviews, thematic analysis.
Findings:
- Testing benefits: "diagnostic confirmation, risk information, and better preparation for the future" — diagnosis-era information explicitly linked by patients to future preparation. → RQ3

## W2778968503 — Prem Senthil et al 2017, J Patient-Reported Outcomes
QoL issues in retinal diseases (hereditary vs acquired), qualitative. Verified (EPMC PMC5934910). `[peer-reviewed/qual-interview/full-text]`
Appraisal: n=79 (32 hereditary incl. 23 RP, median 57, mostly employed; 47 acquired, median 73). Direct HRD-vs-ARD contrast is the value.
Findings:
- Nine QoL domains; hereditary group's dominant issue is ACTIVITY LIMITATION and emotional negativity (frustration, anxiety, depression) vs acquired group's health concerns + passive acceptance. → RQ6, RQ1

- Employment a critical concern for the working-age hereditary group (63% working). → RQ6

## W4296446098 — Simonelli et al 2022, BMJ Open
Narrative medicine study of IRDs: patients, caregivers, clinicians, Italy. Verified (EPMC PMC9486281). `[peer-reviewed/qual-narrative/full-text]`
Appraisal: 8 patients + 8 caregivers + 11 clinicians' parallel charts + 6 professionals; small, specialised centres.
Findings:
- 75% of caregivers experienced hopelessness at diagnosis; couple crises and parental guilt over heritability documented. → RQ3, RQ1
- Patient–caregiver misalignment on functional limitation severity — family members misread the gradient. → RQ1, RQ5
- 25% of patients report uncertainty about progression as the live emotional issue; yet 72% report good QoL — adaptation happens despite absent therapeutic options. → RQ1, RQ6
- Clinicians themselves report helplessness (30%); early psychological support at diagnosis called critical; communication style shapes hopelessness. → RQ3, RQ4
PARKED: narrative-medicine methodology advocacy.

## W4408734060 — Ng et al 2025, Disability & Health Journal
Qualitative SR: IRD lived experiences (patients, families, caregivers). Verified (LSHTM accepted-manuscript PDF, read in full). `[peer-reviewed/systematic-review/full-text]`
Appraisal: PRISMA + Cochrane-Campbell qual-synthesis; PROSPERO CRD42024509430; 3,522 screened → 20 studies, 474 participants, 6 countries (2004–2023); dual-screened, CASP-appraised (all low risk of bias except one), no funding/COI. Strongest-conduct review in the corpus.
Findings:
- Theme (i) independence/work/coping: night vision + peripheral loss → mobility, fear of falling, social/role limits; employment abandoned when tired/unsupported ("It brought my work career really to a halt"); work is dual — fulfilment AND "constant reminder of limitations". → RQ1, RQ6
- Theme (ii) families "hills and valleys": parental guilt over transmission ("I gave this to him…"), witnessing burden, uncertainty; social support "both source of strength and a necessary element for coping". → RQ1, RQ5
- Theme (iii) genetic-screening ambivalence: "do you want to know that something may happen to you in 25 years' time?" — knowing-the-future is itself contested terrain. → RQ3
- Discussion: psychological support "should be provided throughout the care pathway"; systems should integrate mental-health professionals into diagnostic pathways; facilitate peer-support connections for "exchange of adaptive strategies". → RQ4, RQ5
- STATED GAPS (feed Pass 4 "cannot answer"): effectiveness of different coping strategies and "outcomes of differing support interventions" uninvestigated; families/caregivers underexplored (2/20 studies); no phenomenological studies pre-2024; high-income countries only; financial/socio-economic dimensions absent from primary studies. → RQ2-gap, RQ5-gap

## W1944204354 — Spiegel, De Bel & Steverink 2016, Disability and Rehabilitation 38(7):627–636
"Keeping up appearances": identity concealment at work, degenerative eye conditions. Verified (publisher PDF via U. Groningen repository, read pp.1–8; OpenAlex venue/year metadata was wrong — corrected here). `[peer-reviewed/qual-grounded-theory/full-text]`
Appraisal: n=36, ≥40y, RP or Stargardt ≥10y, US foundation recruitment, saturation, dual coding (insider+outsider coder; first author is visually impaired — reflexivity addressed).
Findings:
- Five passing/concealment career patterns: (1) no career adjustment, concealed throughout; (2) revealed after adjusting career plans; (3) increasingly open over career; (4) career planning + always open; (5) limited planning + always open. → RQ1
- Less planning + more concealment → "more stress and lower levels of self-acceptance"; more planning for deterioration + openness → higher self-acceptance, fewer career obstacles. Only source that operationalises *preparation* and links it to wellbeing (observational, no causality). → RQ2
- Deferral verbatim (pattern 1): "the guy [doctor] said I had 30 years, so I figured out the math… I said 30 years is good enough. I'll worry about it at 55." → RQ1, RQ2
- Concealment strain: "I was literally walking into walls and I would not go out with anybody after work… Very stressed out that they're gonna discover me any day now." → RQ1
- Normalizing behaviours = aid refusal: functionally blind participant — "I will not take a dog and will not take a cane… I basically hold [a friend's arm]" — appearing normal preferred over functioning better. → RQ1, RQ2
- Fear of workplace discrimination is the stated concealment motive; several left work rather than disclose. → RQ6
PARKED: ADA-era legal context (US-specific).

## W2167316384 — Thurston 2010, Br J Visual Impairment
