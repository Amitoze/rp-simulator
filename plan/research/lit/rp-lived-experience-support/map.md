## Synthesis per review question

### RQ1 — What blocks preparation? (lived experience of the gradient)

The literature converges on the frame's blocker list and adds a
mechanism the frame implied but didn't name: **adjustment is identity
work along a gradient, and every blocker is a way of staying on the
"sighted" side of it.**

- Adjustment to RP is contingent on an identity shift from sighted to
  visually-impaired person; people distribute across sighted /
  in-transition / VI self-identifications (C1)
  [peer-reviewed/qual-interview/abstract-only — Hayeems05; **needs
  full-text confirmation**], replicated in embodiment/biographical-
  disruption terms [Ng-IPA25/abstract-only] and in transition-stage
  models [Thurston10a,b/abstract-only].
- Concealment and "normalizing behaviours" are active, effortful
  strategies — refusing cane/guide dog to appear normal, hiding at
  work — and they carry measured costs: more stress, lower
  self-acceptance (C2) [peer-reviewed/qual-grounded-theory/full-text —
  Spiegel16]. The dossier's "adaptations that shrink life" appears
  across sources as activity avoidance (C5) [full-text: PremSenthil17,
  Lange21, Green20].
- Deferral is documented behaviour, not just self-report: delayed cane
  use despite benefit [Garip19/full-text], visual acuity at
  rehabilitation intake worse than at recruitment interview months
  earlier [Overbury11/abstract-only], "I'll worry about it at 55"
  [Spiegel16/full-text]. Deferral is also CO-PRODUCED by services:
  ophthalmologists refer only when patients feel ready
  [Stolwijk23/full-text]. (C3)
- Anxiety in RP is anticipatory: it does NOT track current
  acuity/function (depression does), it tracks perceived
  general-health/role threat [Sainohira18/full-text], and it is
  phenotype-specific to the function people expect to lose next
  [Lacy20-MVAQ/full-text]. Uncertainty of a variable, decades-long
  trajectory is itself a named stressor (C4, C18) [Bakir25,
  PremSenthil17, Simonelli22].
- What the frame REJECTED is also absent here: no source reports
  "lack of permission" as a blocker, and information-availability
  appears as *disjointedness and absent guidance*, not absence of
  facts — consistent with the kept frame over rival frame A.

**Conflicts:** none substantive within RQ1. The sampled populations
skew late-stage and charity-recruited (Garip's own caveat), i.e. the
non-engaged mid-journey adult — our protagonist — is systematically
under-represented in the very literature about them.

### RQ2 — Anticipatory adaptation and its timing

**This is the map's hole, now confirmed from three directions.**
(1) Two dedicated searches returned zero on-topic indexed results
(search-log Q6, Q20). (2) The 18-concept matrix's only thin column is
C16 — seven sources, all indirect. (3) The strongest review in the
corpus independently names "outcomes of differing support
interventions" as unstudied [Ng-SR25/full-text].

What the indirect evidence supports:
- Demanding non-visual skills are learnable in mid-life: click-based
  echolocation trained to near-expert levels in blind AND sighted
  adults aged 21–79, with daily-life benefit at follow-up
  [peer-reviewed/training-study/abstract-only — Norman21].
- Planning-for-deterioration correlates with higher wellbeing and
  fewer career obstacles; concealment-without-planning with stress
  (observational, no causal direction) [Spiegel16/full-text].
- Rehabilitation-from-diagnosis is the stated clinical ideal — and the
  same source says the ideal "remains underimplemented in routine
  clinical settings" [Murro23/full-text].
- One small controlled trial of visual training in RP exists
  [Chacón-López13/abstract-only]; one 1997 braille case series
  [deprioritised:braille — timing signal only].

**Bottom line: no study asks whether learning adaptation skills
BEFORE central-vision loss changes outcomes for RP adults. The
deferral clause of the kept frame ("always locally rational") is
neither confirmed nor refuted by evidence — the experiment has not
been run.**

### RQ3 — Diagnosis-era support → later preparedness

Two well-evidenced halves that never meet:
- Diagnosis-era experience is bad: shock/denial at diagnosis;
  clinician communication style materially shapes the emotional
  response; people wanted "the opportunity to talk about their
  feelings at the time of diagnosis" [Garip19/full-text]; diagnosis
  named traumatic across the meta-synthesis [Nyman11/abstract-only];
  74% of a 2024 UK survey got no emotional-support signposting
  [grey/AACODS:caution — G2-RNIB24].
- Diagnosis-era information is explicitly linked BY PATIENTS to
  preparation: genetic testing valued for "better preparation for the
  future" [Combs13a/full-text], "mental preparation for the future"
  [Inaba22/abstract-only]; alongside real ambivalence about knowing
  ("do you want to know that something may happen to you in 25 years'
  time?" [Ng-SR25/full-text]).

**But the weld-watch question — does diagnosis-era support measurably
change mid-journey preparedness — has NO longitudinal evidence
anywhere in the corpus.** The closest is retrospective: early social
support made adaptation easier in participants' own accounts
[Garip19/full-text], and later-life loss may be psychologically
harder than early-life loss [Demmin20/full-text, indirect]. The
dossier's parked causal belief stays a belief.

### RQ4 — Service orientation vs the progressive-partial population

The best-evidenced question. The claim "services skew toward total
blindness" is SUPPORTED IN A REFINED FORM: services are
**event-coded and endpoint-coded, not gradient-coded**.

- Entry bottleneck, quantified: 33% of low-vision patients unaware
  rehabilitation exists; of those newly referred, only 56% engage
  [Overbury11/abstract-only]; once referred, attendance can be high
  (97%) — the bottleneck is upstream in awareness, referral behaviour
  and self-perceived need [Matti10/abstract-only]; physician-side
  misconceptions mirror it [Adam07/abstract-only]; readiness-gated
  referral defers entry [Stolwijk23/full-text]. (C7)
- Blindness-coding at the door: "I'm welcomed by a blind woman, so it
  could get even worse" [Stolwijk23/full-text]; peer-support cultures
  model a *blind* life to newcomers [Botha24/abstract-only]; aids
  read as identity markers, so partial-vision users refuse them
  [Spiegel16/full-text]. (C8)
- Emotional support gap along the whole pathway: "dire, utterly dire"
  [Trott23/full-text]; up to 91% of VI patients with mental-health
  problems untreated [Demmin20/full-text]; counselling provision
  patchy and evidence-free [Nyman10/abstract-only]; psychosocial
  referral inconsistent [Cross22/full-text]. (C6)
- The gradient is untracked: support needs change with deterioration
  but services lack systematic follow-up to catch it
  [Thetford11/abstract-only]; patients' worst adjustment domain is
  their orientation to the health-care system itself
  [Jangra07/abstract-only].
- The multidisciplinary/coordinated ideal exists on paper
  [Murro23/full-text; Herse05/abstract-only; Fontenot17/UNREAD
  practice pattern] and is described as underimplemented.
- Recorded null: no Vision Australia / Guide Dogs research reports
  found (absence of evidence — their service design remains
  uncharacterised in published sources).

### RQ5 — Peer support (kept over challenge)

- Peers "who have successfully adjusted" are a named adjustment
  facilitator [Nyman11/abstract-only]; support groups help but are
  fit-sensitive (age-mismatch failure case) [Garip19/full-text];
  online community does double duty — emotional disclosure AND
  practical strategy exchange, enabled by anonymity
  [Ng-Reddit25/full-text].
- Structured, time-boxed group models existed by 1981/1994
  [McCulloh94, Galler81/abstract-only] — existence proof for "guided,
  paced" community formats; no effectiveness evidence then or since
  [Nyman10/abstract-only; Ng-SR25/full-text names the gap].
- Critical counterweight: peer communities are culture-shapers, not
  neutral goods — comparison, othering and surveillance operate
  inside them, and they model a particular (blind-coded) life
  [Botha24/abstract-only]. Relevant to C8: an unguided community can
  itself be binary-coded.

### RQ6 — Most significant challenges of the central-vision-loss transition

The densest evidence (C11–C13, 11-source C12). As predicted at
protocol time, the answer is a weighted map, not a ranking:

- **Stage-resolved difficulty structure** [Latham16/abstract-only,
  Rasch DIF]: earlier/partial stage → light extremes (poor AND bright
  light), peripheral obstacles; later/registered stage → finding
  products, public transport. Central-vision task loss (reading,
  faces) defines the feared next stage [Szlyk97, Ehrlich23,
  Green20/full-text: reading 17/21, driving 15/21].
- **QoL collapses before acuity does**: NEI-VFQ 68.4 vs 90.1 controls
  in RP patients with preserved acuity ≥0.7 [Sugawara10/
  abstract-only] — quantitative core of "disabled while looking
  normal".
- **Mental-health burden ~2× population**: stress 51.9% vs 29.4%,
  depressive mood 34.8% vs 17.1% [Kim13/abstract-only]; anxiety 37%,
  depression 26% [Sainohira18/full-text]; depression tracks PERCEIVED
  function, not measured vision [Hahm08/abstract-only]. 92% say RP
  affects their mental health [Cross22/full-text]. ⚠ Suicide-risk
  claim (SMR 2.61) remains narrative-review-cited, primary unchased —
  not usable until verified [Chivers21/full-text].
- **Career cliff in mid-journey**: employment 61.5% vs 83.9%
  [Cross22/full-text]; early retirement 39.8% at ≥40y, 50% at ≥50y
  [Zehe-Lindau25/abstract-only]; unemployment 64% in one US sample
  [Humphries24/abstract-only]. Conflict, stated: employment protects
  against depression in Japan [Sainohira18] but was non-predictive in
  a small US sample [Humphries24] — unresolved, populations differ.
- **Cross-cutting burdens**: communicating vision loss to others is
  the single hardest emotional task [Latham15/abstract-only];
  fatigue is a distinct burden (C17) [Schakel17/abstract-only];
  uncertainty/fluctuation pervade (C4/C18).
- Methods caveat for the whole cell: pre-2020 QoL data used
  instruments now known to miss IRD-specific experience
  [Lacy22/abstract-only] — older magnitudes are directionally
  reliable, precisely uncertain.

## What the literature cannot answer
(→ becomes dossier §2 open evidence requests; phrased as askable)

1. **The timing question (RQ2, empty):** For an RP adult with usable
   central vision, does learning non-visual/adaptation skills BEFORE
   they're needed change outcomes (skill retention, distress at
   transition, continued employment)? — *Interview:* "Have you ever
   tried to learn a skill for vision you still had? What happened —
   or what stopped you?"
2. **The deferral trigger (RQ1/RQ2):** deferral is documented; what
   BREAKS it is not. — *Interview:* "What made you finally start
   [cane / screen reader / disclosure]? What would have made you
   start two years earlier?"
3. **The weld-watch causal link (RQ3):** does diagnosis-era support
   measurably change mid-journey preparedness? No longitudinal
   evidence exists. — *Interview (retrospective proxy):* "What did
   you get at diagnosis, and can you trace anything you did later to
   it?"
4. **Structured vs unstructured peer support (RQ5):** formats exist,
   effectiveness unstudied since 1994-era descriptions. —
   *Interview:* "What has the community actually changed about how
   you adapt — and what has it made worse?"
5. **Where mid-journey adults actually get guidance (RQ4):** formal
   services are mapped; the informal channel mix (YouTube, Facebook,
   Reddit, one-off OTs) is essentially unstudied bar one Reddit
   analysis. — *Interview:* "Walk me through where your last three
   adaptation ideas came from."
6. **Whether AU sector services are gradient-capable (RQ4):** no
   published characterisation of Vision Australia / Guide Dogs
   service design for partial vision. — *Ask the providers, not the
   literature.*
7. **Literature-chase, not interview:** the suicide-risk primary
   behind Chivers21's SMR 2.61 claim.
