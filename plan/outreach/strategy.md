## The verdict in five lines

1. **The people you need come from rooms, not inboxes.** In every
   comparable founder story the first collaborator came from a
   convening (hackathon, startup weekend, accelerator cohort, a lab that
   lent a desk) — none came from cold email. `[verified-fetch, n=11
   cases]` And the outreach literature agrees: cold email is the one
   channel where people *over*-estimate their persuasiveness (in-person
   asks ~34× more effective, both predicted equal). `[peer-reviewed]`
2. **Melbourne is unusually good ground for the next 12 months.** The
   world low-vision research conference (Vision 2027) is at MCEC in
   September 2027, organised by Lauren Ayton — the person already at the
   top of your door list. The national AT conference is at the MCG in
   November 2026. Monash MATS is recruiting PhD candidates with
   disability, deadline 31 October 2026.
3. **"Funding so I don't have to work" is a 12–18 month project, not a
   3-month one.** Only five programs found pay a *person* to live
   (Kenneth Myer $180k/12 months, Snow $100–200k/yr, Paul Ramsay $250k,
   Echoing Green US$100k, a PhD stipend ~$34–39k). The first four open
   mid-to-late 2027 and want evidence you don't yet have. The PhD
   stipend is the only one with a live deadline, and it is a pay cut.
4. **The simulator is the calling card; AR is the pitch; the interview
   programme is the give.** The simulator is exactly the "demo that
   provokes a reaction" pattern; simulators as products, though, are
   institution-owned awareness assets that nobody funds standalone.
5. **Ask for advice, not partnership.** Advice-seeking raises perceived
   competence with experts on hard problems `[peer-reviewed]`; pitch
   language lowers reply rates `[vendor-benchmark]`; and the RP peer
   communities ban unapproved surveys outright.

Confidence: high on 1, 2, 5 (multiple independent sources); medium on 3
(program pages change; amounts verified, 2027 dates inferred from
annual patterns); 4 is my synthesis.

## Where I disagree with the brief, or would sharpen it

- **The drafted Vision Australia email is the right words in the wrong
  instrument.** It is ~250 words with two asks; the evidence says
  openers under ~50 words with one ask, and that the ask should be a
  15-minute call, not the collaboration itself. Keep the draft as your
  talking points for the call. Cut the email to the template below.
- **Lead with Monash and CERA, not Vision Australia.** VA is your
  distribution and interview channel (weeks, vetted). Monash MATS and
  CERA are the doors that convert into stipend, ethics cover, Aria
  hardware, and grant hosting — and one of them has a deadline in seven
  weeks. Your own outreach note already sequences VA behind the cheap
  frame test; this extends that.
- **The cheap frame test cannot be a Facebook/Reddit post.** r/Blind
  bans surveys without university ethics approval and requires mod
  approval for any project mention; Facebook RP groups are
  admin-gated. The test survives as *conversations*, recruited through
  Guide Dogs Victoria peer groups, Blind Sports Victoria, Retina
  Australia's Telelink, or a Talking Vision segment — relationship-
  forward, paid (~$50–65/hr), and framed as talking, not research.
- **"Funding so I don't have to work" needs a bridge income decision
  now, not later.** Realistic 2027 options are: (a) full-time PhD
  stipend from ~March 2027 (~$34–39k, tax-free), (b) part-time day job +
  part-time PhD (half stipend), (c) day job through to a Kenneth Myer /
  Snow decision in late 2027. The founder cases say the employer-as-
  incubator route (Seeing AI, AI Suitcase, Soundscape) is real — worth
  one conversation with your current employer before choosing.
- **Hardware is the trap in this field.** Every comparable hardware
  venture took 5–10 years and is dead (OxSight, liquidated 2023) or
  still pre-revenue (Glide, AI Suitcase). Software on consumer AR
  (Milan group's XREAL+iPhone, Retiplus) is the survivable framing.
  Say "software for glasses people already own" in every pitch.

## The playbook — ten rules, each with its source

| # | Rule | Why (evidence tag) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Written contact asks for **15 minutes live**; the real ask happens on the call | Roghanizad & Bohns 2017/2022 `[peer-reviewed]` |
| 2 | Opener ≤ 50 words, one ask, subject 36–50 chars, LinkedIn in signature | Milkman 2015 `[peer-reviewed]`; Backlinko/Gong `[vendor-benchmark]` |
| 3 | Frame as **advice on a hard, specific problem in their work** — never "partner/collaborate/ROI" | Brooks, Gino & Schweitzer 2015 `[peer-reviewed]` |
| 4 | Pre-write two follow-ups (day +4, +11), each adding something new; stop at three | Backlinko, Woodpecker `[vendor-benchmark]` |
| 5 | Double your ask count; expect ~2× the yes-rate your gut predicts (live asks) | Flynn & Lake 2008 `[peer-reviewed]` |
| 6 | One message a day for two weeks, not a batch — the recalibration is the point | Sandstrom et al. 2022 `[peer-reviewed]` |
| 7 | Before any cold email, reconnect 10 dormant ties and ask them for advice | Levin, Walter & Murnighan 2011 `[peer-reviewed]` |
| 8 | Prefer second-degree contacts with a few mutual connections | Rajkumar et al. 2022 `[peer-reviewed]` |
| 9 | With clinicians: respect clinic time, demo in their room, send a one-paragraph outcome note after | PMC7718701 `[peer-reviewed, qual]` |
| 10 | With peers: mods/admins first, pay at consumer rates, give them a meaning-shaping role | r/Blind rules, PWDA, HCNSW 2025 `[factual-source]` |

## Templates

**Opener (≤ 60 words, one ask).** Adjust the bracketed parts only.

> Subject: RP, a working simulator, and one question about [their topic]
>
> Hi [Name] — I have retinitis pigmentosa and built a browser simulator
> of how RP vision actually works (link). I'm now looking at [their
> specific topic] and have one hard question I think only you can
> answer: [question]. Worth 15 minutes on a call in the next fortnight?
>
> [Name] · [LinkedIn] · [phone]

**Follow-up 1 (day +4):** one new thing — a screenshot, a finding from
your lit map, a mutual contact's name. **Follow-up 2 (day +11):** "Happy
to close the loop if this isn't a fit — one line back is plenty." Stop.

**After any call:** a one-paragraph note the same day — what you heard,
what you'll do, when you'll report back. This is the clinician
facilitator the literature names (never hearing what happened is their
top complaint).

**Peer interview ask (via a group admin or facilitator, never a post):**
> I have RP and I'm talking to a few people who are losing vision but
> not yet blind, about what has and hasn't helped them prepare. It's a
> 45-minute conversation, paid [$50] as a voucher, not a survey, not
> recorded unless you want it to be. Would you put this to the group?

## How you'll know it's working

- **Two-arm test, first fortnight:** 10 advice-framed openers vs 10
  partner-framed openers, each with two follow-ups; count replies at
  day 14. The evidence predicts advice wins. If it doesn't, the framing
  advice above is wrong for this audience — say so in DECISIONS.md.
- **Room test:** leave AATC with ≥5 named people who agreed to receive a
  note. Fewer than 3 means the pitch, not the room, is the problem.
- **Money test, 31 Oct:** a MATS decision either way. Deferral is the
  documented failure mode in your own dossier — this is the same
  mechanism, one level up.

## What would change my mind

- If Marriott or Ayton say a lived-experience PhD is the wrong vehicle,
  the critical path shifts to Remarkable → entity → Snow, and the
  timeline for a stipend moves to 2028.
- If the frame-test conversations cluster on cost or denial rather than
  overwhelm/deferral, the product horizon changes and so does the pitch.
- If the Milan group or Retiplus publish a consumer-glasses product for
  RP before Vision 2027, the AR pitch becomes "Australian trial site",
  not "new approach" — which may be easier to fund, not harder.
