## What to ask for (separate the two asks — don't bundle)

1. **Collaboration ask:** a 30-minute call with someone in service
   design / client experience / research. Framing: you're a VA client
   with RP doing evidence-based work on the *mid-journey* gap — people
   losing vision, not yet blind — and you have a literature map that
   names specific service-pathway failure points (awareness → referral
   → readiness leakage; blindness-coded entry). You're offering
   findings, not just requesting favours. This is also where evidence
   request 6 gets asked directly.
2. **Recruitment ask:** how to reach clients for interviews — the
   research-listing page, their newsletter (FYI), or facilitated
   introductions. Expect a vetting step; have a one-paragraph project
   description and interview outline ready.

**Sequencing note:** the dossier's cheap frame test (2–3 conversations
via Facebook/YouTube communities you already know) should NOT wait on
Vision Australia — org timelines are weeks, and the cheap test is
specifically designed to need no institution. Run both in parallel.

Notes on the draft: leads with client status + lived experience (your

## Retina Australia variant

Same email works with the first paragraph swapped — you're not a

broker member interviews for researchers. Consider joining as a member
first ($ small) — it converts the ask from outsider to member.

## Interview-programme practicalities (for when a channel opens)

- **Consent:** even for non-academic product research — one-page plain
  -language sheet (purpose, recording, storage, withdrawal, anonymity)
  and verbal consent on tape. VA/Retina Australia will likely ask to
  see it before circulating anything.
- **Accessible formats:** any recruitment text and consent sheet must
  work with screen readers — plain text or tagged docs, no scanned PDFs.
- **The dossier's warning stands:** charity-recruited samples
  over-select the *engaged*; the frame's protagonist is the
  non-engaged mid-journey adult. VA/RA channels complement — not
  replace — the Facebook/YouTube route for reaching the non-engaged.
