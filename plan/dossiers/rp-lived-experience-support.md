## 1 Frame

### Problem as first stated (verbatim, 2026-08-21)

> The problem as I see it is that currently there are no great tools for
> helping someone that gets diagnosed with retinitis pigmentosa to help
> them navigate the issues of gradual vision loss. My experience was that
> I was diagnosed with the condition at 16, could no longer drive, but
> other than that no follow on support.
>
> Parents and the person diagnosed can largely be in denial of the
> condition because there is enough residual central vision to do most
> things, and it does not appear from the outside that you have a
> disability.
>
> This denial is an issue in two ways: 1) the patient is often not
> supported with the stress of their situation, and 2) can lead to a
> failure to prepare for loss of central vision, and 3) can lead to
> adpations along the way where the patient avoids activities as a way of
> coping with the loss (eg I stopped playing sports because I could no
> longer track players or the ball - this makes me seem normal in a sense
> as the disability is not being activated or seen as a limitation).

### Abstraction ladder
(in progress — Pass 1 interrogation underway)

**Raw material from Q1 ("why does this matter?"), user's open thoughts, 2026-08-21:**

- **Family/cultural denial:** parents (educated, traditional Indian family)
  discouraged cane and guide dog; shame attached to disability → emphasis on
  covering it up. User only adopted aids via outsiders (Vision Australia,
  Guide Dogs). [user-experience]
- **Illegibility to others:** when using the cane, strangers assumed
  "pretending to be blind" — people model vision as binary (see / not see).
  User lacked an in-the-moment tool to show how they see: recognises a face
  at 100 m yet struggles with mobility. Reported as common among others with
  RP. [user-experience; prevalence claim untagged — needs evidence]
- **Self-directed adaptation, mid-journey (~40, central vision now at
  risk):** discovering adaptations alone. Services (Guide Dogs, Vision
  Australia) skew toward total blindness; partial-vision guidance is
  disjointed (YouTube, Facebook groups, one-off OT engagements). Wants a
  central, organised, active community of specific challenges + replicable
  solutions. [user-experience; "services skew total-blindness" needs
  evidence beyond one person]

**Emerging mess (rung above):** support infrastructure and social
understanding are *binary* (sighted vs blind), while RP is a decades-long
gradient — so the person on the gradient is illegible to family, strangers,
services, and partly to themselves.

**Candidate extracted problems (rungs below):**
1. Newly-diagnosed teen + family: denial/shame window, no follow-on support.
2. Legibility: no in-the-moment way to show others how partial vision works.
   ⚠ solution-in-hand: this is the frame the existing simulator serves.
3. Mid-journey partial-vision adaptation: no organised, community-backed,
   replicable guidance for people losing (not lost) vision.

**User's extraction (Q2, 2026-08-21):** problem 3 — "preparation for
central vision loss, the current situation I am in."

**Weld watch:** the user keeps diagnosis attached as "a really important
entry point, empowering families at that point… so that the stress and
preparation at the point of central vision loss is less." That is a causal
*belief* (diagnosis-time empowerment → easier mid-journey), not an
observation — and it re-welds problem 1 onto problem 3. Parked as an open
evidence request (→ §2): would diagnosis-era support measurably change
mid-journey preparedness? Entry-point choice is a distribution/solution
concern, not part of the problem statement.

### Rival frames (attacked 2026-08-21)

- **A — Permission frame** ("the blocker is permission to prepare, not
  guidance"): REJECTED for the chosen protagonist. User: "doesn't ring true
  now, I definitely give myself permission." True of the 16-year-old and
  possibly the newly diagnosed (→ sibling 1), not of the mid-journey adult.
  The attack still paid: it surfaced the *real* blockers — emotional
  overwhelm, not knowing where to begin, steep learning curve, and rational
  deferral ("I can continue as I am for now").
- **B — Community frame** ("the problem is finding each other; content is a
  byproduct of connection"): PARTIALLY ABSORBED, not kept whole. User
  confirmed a library/document pile fails against emotional overload, and
  that peers who've walked the path are essential — but the surviving core
  is the *unguided path*, with community as a necessary property of any
  answer, not the problem itself. (User's phrasing here — "intuitive flow,
  slowly unlocks a skill" — is solution language; parked for /roadmap, kept
  out of the statement.)
- **C — Service-misfit frame** ("fix Vision Australia, don't build"):
  REJECTED as the frame — not mutually exclusive with acting directly; user
  intends to collaborate with Vision Australia. Retained as a *channel*
  fact for §3, not a frame.

### Kept frame

> Adults with partial vision who are losing more of it (RP, mid-journey)
> fail to prepare for central vision loss — not for lack of information or
> permission, but because adaptation has no guided, emotionally manageable
> path: the material is disjointed, the starting point unclear, the
> learning curve steep, and deferral is always locally rational while
> residual central vision still suffices.

**What-ELSE test (solution-in-hand check):** things other than the existing
simulator that would serve this frame: a cohort-based adaptation program, a
peer-mentor matching service, an OT-designed staged curriculum run with
Vision Australia, a structured community (guided, paced) — multiple
non-simulator answers fit, so the frame is NOT reverse-engineered from the
built tool. ⚠️ Corollary, stated plainly: **the existing rp-simulator does
not serve the kept frame.** It serves sibling 2 (legibility) and possibly
sibling 1 (family denial at diagnosis). This is a scoping fact for the
project, not a kill of the simulator.

### Siblings parked

1. **diagnosis-window-support** — newly-diagnosed teen + family: denial,
   shame (incl. cultural shame in traditional/immigrant families), no
   follow-on support at the entry point. Carries the untested belief that
   diagnosis-era support eases mid-journey preparation (→ §2 open request).
2. **partial-vision-legibility** — no in-the-moment way to show another
   person how partial vision works ("pretending to be blind"). The existing
   rp-simulator lives here.

### Move forward — cheap frame test

One conversation each with 2–3 other mid-journey partial-vision adults
(reachable via the RP Facebook groups / YouTube communities the user
already knows): ask, without leading, "what has stopped you preparing for
further loss?" The frame predicts answers clustering on overwhelm /
where-to-begin / deferral — NOT on "I couldn't find information" or "I
wasn't allowed." Also predicts they describe current resources as
disjointed. If their blockers cluster elsewhere (e.g. cost, denial,
permission), the frame needs revision.

## 2 Evidence
(lit-map digest, ratified 2026-08-22 — full resolution in

### What the literature establishes for the kept frame

- **The blockers are real and mechanised.** Adjustment to progressive
  loss is identity work along a gradient (sighted → in-transition →
  VI); concealment, aid-refusal and deferral are ways of staying on
  the sighted side, and concealment-without-planning measurably
  costs stress and self-acceptance.
  [peer-reviewed/qual-grounded-theory/full-text — Spiegel16;
  peer-reviewed/qual/abstract-only — Hayeems05 (needs full-text)]
- **Deferral is documented behaviour and partly service-co-produced**:
  people arrive at rehabilitation with worse vision than months
  earlier; clinicians refer only when patients feel "ready".
  [peer-reviewed/abstract-only — Overbury11;
  peer-reviewed/qual/full-text — Stolwijk23]
- **"No permission" and "no information" do not appear as blockers**
  anywhere in the corpus — the frame's rejection of the permission
  frame and its "disjointed, unguided" characterisation both hold.
  [my-synthesis over the corpus]
- **Anxiety in RP is anticipatory**: it tracks the function people
  expect to lose next, not current vision; depression tracks
  *perceived* (not measured) function. Mental-health burden ≈2×
  general population. [peer-reviewed/full-text — Sainohira18, Lacy20;
  abstract-only — Hahm08, Kim13]
- **QoL collapses while acuity is still good** (NEI-VFQ 68 vs 90 at
  acuity ≥0.7) and the challenge structure is stage-resolved (light
  extremes/obstacles early; reading, faces, products, transport
  later); hardest emotional task = communicating vision loss;
  mid-journey career cliff (early retirement 40–50%).
  [peer-reviewed/abstract-only — Sugawara10, Latham15/16,
  Zehe-Lindau25; full-text — Cross22, Green20]
- **Services are event-coded and endpoint-coded, not gradient-coded**
  (refined form of the "skew total-blindness" claim): 33% unaware
  services exist; blindness-coded identity at the service door;
  emotional support along the pathway "dire"; no follow-up tracks
  deterioration. [peer-reviewed/full-text — Trott23, Stolwijk23,
  Demmin20; abstract-only — Overbury11, Thetford11;
  grey/AACODS:caution — RNIB 2024: 74% no emotional-support
  signposting]
- **Peer support is a facilitator but not a neutral good** — helpful,
  fit-sensitive, effectiveness never studied; unguided communities
  model a blind-coded life to newcomers. [full-text — Garip19,
  Ng-Reddit25, Ng-SR25; abstract-only — Nyman11, Botha24]
- ⚠ Quarantined until primary chased: suicide-risk SMR 2.61 claim
  (narrative-review-cited only, Chivers21).

### OPEN EVIDENCE REQUESTS (= the literature's cannot-answers;
interview-ready phrasings in map.md §cannot-answer)

1. Does learning non-visual skills BEFORE loss change outcomes for RP
   adults? (zero direct studies — the frame's central question is
   unstudied)
2. What breaks deferral — what actually triggers people to start
   adapting?
3. Does diagnosis-era support change mid-journey preparedness? (the
   §1 weld-watch belief; no longitudinal evidence exists)
4. Structured vs unstructured peer support — what does structure add?
5. Where do mid-journey adults actually get adaptation guidance
   (informal channel mix — essentially unstudied)?
6. Are Vision Australia / Guide Dogs services gradient-capable? (no
   published characterisation; ask the providers)
7. (literature-chase) primary source behind the suicide-risk claim.

## 3 Landscape
(evidenced input from the lit map, ratified 2026-08-22 — /frame Pass 3
still interrogates this; tags as in §2)

What exists today for a mid-journey RP adult, per the literature:

- **Clinical ophthalmology** — diagnosis and monitoring; experienced
  as information-poor ("expected more understanding and support from
  their doctors"), communication-style-sensitive, with clinicians
  themselves reporting helplessness. [full-text — Chivers21,
  Simonelli22]
- **Low-vision rehabilitation services** — effective once entered,
  but entry is the failure point (awareness → referral → readiness →
  attendance leakage at every step) and they are blindness-coded to
  the partial-vision eye. No systematic re-engagement as vision
  changes. [full-text — Stolwijk23; abstract-only — Overbury11,
  Matti10, O'Connor08, Thetford11]
- **Genetic testing / counselling** — valued, explicitly hired for
  "mental preparation for the future"; workforce thin (89% of IRD
  genetic counselors say MD:GC ratio too high); support around it
  time-bounded to diagnosis. [full-text — Combs13a; abstract-only —
  Inaba22, Alabek23]
- **Counselling / mental-health support** — scarce, un-signposted,
  evidence-free for this population; up to 91% of VI patients with
  mental-health problems untreated. [full-text — Trott23, Demmin20;
  abstract-only — Nyman10]
- **Peer communities (offline + online)** — the de-facto guidance
  channel; double duty (emotional + practical strategy exchange), but
  unguided, fit-sensitive, and culture-shaping toward blind-coded
  identities. Structured group formats existed by the 1980s–90s and
  were never evaluated. [full-text — Ng-Reddit25; abstract-only —
  Botha24, McCulloh94]
- **The multidisciplinary coordinated pathway** — exists as published
  ideal (rehab-from-diagnosis, coordinator roles) and is
  "underimplemented in routine clinical settings" — i.e. the guided
  path the frame says is missing is *prescribed but not delivered*.
  [full-text — Murro23; abstract-only — Herse05]

Vision Australia; their service design is unpublished (open request 6).
