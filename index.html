RP Simulator

Retinitis pigmentosa is an inherited condition in which the light-sensing cells of the retina slowly die, usually from the edges of vision inward. Clinical descriptions say tunnel vision and night blindness. They rarely say what the lost field actually looks like. This simulator filters live camera or street footage through one person's visual field, including the part other simulators leave out: the dead region is not black, it is filled in, and it flashes.

  • Through my eyes First person, at a late stage: a small surviving centre, a flickering dead ring, and a few far islands of sight. Drag to look around. Open the experience →
  • What you see, what I see Normal vision beside the simulated field, same footage, same moment. The clearest way to show a sighted person what is missing. Open the comparison →
  • The glance aid A head-worn display drawn into the scene, then seen through the condition: a preview of what an AR aid could put inside the surviving field. Open the AR preview →
All three contain continuous rapid flashing at around 9 Hz. The simulator asks you to confirm before it starts.

This is a portrait, not an average and not a medical model. RP is an umbrella term for thousands of genetic conditions with similar patterns of loss; another person would draw a different picture of their own experience. The imagery is checked against the clinical literature where it exists, and follows the person where it diverges.
